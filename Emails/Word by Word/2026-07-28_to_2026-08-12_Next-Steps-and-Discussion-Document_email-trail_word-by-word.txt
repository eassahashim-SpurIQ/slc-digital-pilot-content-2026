to Numan, Veena, arush, Travis, Gavin, Elliott, anvesha, Anvesha, me

Hi Numan,

Overall, I think the templates are good. One principle I try to follow when doing outreach is to focus less on selling the feature and more on selling the value.

I agree with the amendments you’ve made. The person you’re emailing is an individual, so the messaging needs to resonate with what matters to them. For example, if I’m emailing a CTO, I’d think about their day-to-day priorities: efficiency, risk, budgets, scaling etc. The message should then be built around how we can add value in that context. So, yes, I like that the CFO/COO template focuses on areas such as budgets and financial priorities.

I also agree that we should take out “your name came up on register”. It isn’t personal and there’s no real intent behind that messaging.

Personally, I work on a four-week sequence. 

Something along the lines of:
Week 1 – Introduce the problem/value
Introduce the challenge and why it’s relevant to them.
Week 2 – Relevant insight or challenge
Share an insight around an issue they may be facing.
Week 3 – Social proof/use case
Show how we’ve helped similar teams or organisations.
Week 4 – Simple, direct CTA
Keep it short and make it easy for them to respond.

For the final email, I’d change the subject line to “Please advise.” I’ve found that a simple, slightly curiosity-driven subject line like that can generate a better open rate.

From my two days of SLC, I think the real value of SLC to a business, sits across four key areas:

1. Reduce fraud and financial risk
“How much is fraud costing you today - not just in lost money, but in investigation, remediation, customer compensation and operational resource?”
2. Strengthen security beyond the login
“You can authenticate a customer successfully at 9am, but how confident are you that the person approving a £50,000 payment at 9:15am is still that customer?”
3. Protect the customer experience
“The challenge isn’t just stopping fraud. It’s stopping fraud without making your best customers jump through more hoops.”
4. Improve security without replacing the existing stack
“You don’t need to rip out the security infrastructure you’ve already invested in. SLC adds another layer where you need stronger assurance, without turning it into a multi-year transformation programme.”

I think if we build the outreach around these value points, rather than focusing too heavily on the SLC feature itself, the messaging will resonate much more with the individual we’re targeting.
